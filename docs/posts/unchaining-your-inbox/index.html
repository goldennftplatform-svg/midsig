Unchaining your inbox

Why the From line is still free to forge in 2026, and what MIDSIG does about it.

The chain

Your inbox is the endpoint of a chain nobody asked to be verified. SPF says which servers may send for a domain. DKIM says which relay signed the body. Neither says which message you're looking at actually came from the domain it claims. The From line is decoration. The Message-ID is a suggestion.

The unlock

MIDSIG makes the message identity a cryptographic commitment. One DNS record carries your domain's Ed25519 key. Every message you send signs (domain, Message-ID, timestamp). Any receiver, anywhere, can check it in microseconds — no registry, no CA, no coordination.

The spam part

Signatures kill spoofing but not spam — spammers can sign too. So the header comes with a pricing knob: an optional proof-of-work stamp the receiver prices.

"If you don't own the domain and you can't afford 5 cents, I don't want it."

Burner domains can still pay; legitimate mail pays nothing it can feel. Spam economics breaks at scale because the receiver sets the price.

Adoption without a flag day

$ midsig verify --input mail.eml --required-bits 20
verdict: pass

One receiving domain can start enforcing today and get the full protection for its own users. Senders add a record and a header. No committee, no upgrade window, no waiting on the other 4 billion inboxes. That is the whole trick: make the protocol good enough that it spreads one domain at a time.
